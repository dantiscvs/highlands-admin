Bookings

Forward the confirmation. We read it.

Every trip gets its own import address. Send a booking email to it and the flight, train or hotel comes back as a proposed change you review and accept.

  • Email or paste a confirmation, or upload the PDF.
  • Nothing is written silently — every extracted field is a proposal you approve.
  • Locked fields stay yours; an import can never overwrite something you edited by hand.
  • Seats, references, times and costs land where they belong.

Built for a group, not a solo spreadsheet

The awkward parts of travelling together — who packs what, who paid, who can edit — are the parts Trip Tracker actually handles.

🎒

Not everyone packs everything

One multi-tool between three people, but a rain jacket each. Assign an item to specific people or to everyone, and each person ticks off only their own list.

💸

Settle up without arguments

Log an expense, pick who it was split between, and see the running balance per person per currency. No spreadsheet, no end-of-trip forensics.

👥

Roles that make sense

Owners and editors change the plan. Riders tick their own boxes and log progress. Viewers — a partner at home — just read. Invite by link.

👁️

See what they see

Preview the whole app as a participant in one click, so you know exactly what your group gets before you send the invite.

☑️

Readiness warnings

Nights with no bed booked. Long stages with no resupply. Riders with no tracking link. Warnings, never blockers — you decide what matters.

📷

Photos from the road

Anyone on the trip can post a photo, tagged to a day. They appear on the live page for everyone following at home.

Not only for cyclists

Pick the trip type and the app adapts — which fields appear, how they are labelled, and how pace and arrival times are estimated.

🚴

Cycling

Distance, ascent and surface, with speed modelled per surface type and a climbing rate.

🥾

Hiking & trekking

Terrain instead of surface, and climbing time estimated the way Naismith's rule does it.

🚐

Driving & campervan

No ascent column cluttering the grid. Costs become the headline metric instead of climbing.

🛶

Kayaking & paddling

Water conditions replace surface, with paddling speed set per condition.

🚆

Public transport tours

Distance optional, pace estimates off, logistics front and centre.

🧭

Mixed

Segment types for trips that ride, walk and paddle in the same week.

How it works

From a rough idea to a trip you can run

Create the trip

Start from scratch, from a template, or by uploading a GPX or spreadsheet you already have. Pick the activity type and turn on the modules you need.

Fill in the plan

Edit days in the grid, add stays and transport, drop in sights and resupply stops. Forward booking emails to the trip's import address instead of retyping them.

Bring the group in

Invite people by link, set their roles, assign packing and tasks. Check readiness warnings before you leave.

Run it, and share it

Use Today on the road, log kilometres as you go, post photos. Send one public link to everyone at home and let them follow along.

Common questions

What does it cost?

Trip Tracker is free while it is in early access. We are onboarding the first groups now and would rather have your feedback than your money.

Do the people I travel with need accounts?

Only if they want to tick their own boxes or log expenses. You invite them by link and they sign in with an email — no password to invent. Anyone you just want to follow the trip needs nothing at all: the live page is a plain public link.

Is my address and booking data public?

No. The live page is off until you enable it, and every sensitive field is controlled separately — exact addresses, costs, phone numbers and booking references are each hidden or shown by your choice. You can also put a passcode on the link.

Do I have to retype my bookings?

No. Each trip gets its own email address. Forward a confirmation and the details come back as proposals you accept or reject. You can also paste text or upload a PDF.

Does it work on a phone?

Yes. The planning grid is built for a laptop, but every screen has a card layout that works on a phone, which is what you will actually use on the road.
